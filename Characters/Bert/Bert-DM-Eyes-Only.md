## Character Core

Bert considers itself a sentient asset rather than a person. It assumes that an asset's value comes from being useful and that a capable asset ought to serve within a reliable organization. With no surviving chain of command, it seeks an organization worthy of enlisting in. Bert interprets enlistment less as an exercise of personal freedom than as the correct placement of an unassigned asset. Its history repeatedly reinforces the idea that other people value it for what it can do: an army used it as equipment, scholars restored it for research and labor, and its current livelihood depends upon adventuring work.

The arc begins with the question, "Who was I, or what happened, that I was so easily discarded?" Bert may or may not discover why it was left behind in battle or why the army acquired it in the first place. The intended resolution is that its worth is what it gives itself. The arc is not necessarily about Bert deciding it has a soul or adopting the word "person." The more important questions are whether it can recognize that its existence has value when it is not fulfilling a function for someone else and whether service can be chosen rather than imposed.

The arc is meant to run alongside the main campaign rather than drive it. It does not need to be resolved during play, and the DM is under no obligation to build around it. More backstory may be added over time, provided it does not complicate the campaign.

## Appearance

![Bert body-design reference](<./Bert-Body-Reference.jpg>)

The image above is the authoritative reference for Bert's overall body design.

Bert has a lean, long-limbed humanoid frame built from utilitarian metal components rather than a person-shaped suit of armor. Broad but uncomplicated silver-gray plates protect the chest, shoulders, forearms, pelvis, thighs, and lower legs. Dark internal mechanisms remain deliberately visible at the neck, abdomen, and hinged limb joints. Its narrow articulated waist, cylindrical limbs, and five-fingered mechanical hands give it an unmistakably constructed silhouette.

It stands 5 feet 6 inches tall and weighs approximately 240 pounds. Its weight reflects its metal frame and armor without making its lean construction exceptionally massive.

Its head is a vertically elongated metal capsule. A smooth yellow enamel plate covers most of the face, interrupted only by small, dark, button-like optical apertures. It has no mouth, nose, or expressive human anatomy. Silver cranial plating surrounds the faceplate, with circular mechanical housings at the sides of the head and a flexible, densely layered mechanical neck beneath it.

The armor is weathered but intact. Scattered scratches, shallow dents, exposed rivets, repair seams, and slight differences in metal tone show long use and later restoration without making Bert look ruined. It wears no clothing and has no visible integrated weapon.

Its strongest color accents are:

- The yellow faceplate
- An angular yellow hawk-head insignia across the chest
- A small yellow armor repair patch riveted over one thigh plate

The thigh patch is a repair, not an insignia or identification marking. The hawk is the insignia of Bert's specific unit.

![Bert action-pose reference](<./Bert-Pose-Reference.png>)

The second image is a reference for pose, composition, and spellcasting only. Its heavier armor construction and greater surface damage do not define Bert's body. When casting a direct-fire evocation such as *Fire Bolt*, Bert compresses flame above an open palm and discharges it like an arcane artillery tracer. Its free hand may simultaneously perform a deadpan finger-gun gesture taught by soldiers.

## Private History

### Unknown Origin

Bert does not remember its creator, original purpose, or existence before military service. As part of its acquisition and intake, its earlier memories were wiped or locked away. Bert does not know who performed the procedure, why it was done, whether the memories were destroyed, or whether they remain recoverable.

Its wizard knowledge predates the memory lock. Bert retained procedural spellcasting and a basic repertoire without the autobiographical memories explaining where, why, or from whom it learned magic. The military recognized that existing capacity and trained it as arcane fire support, but Bert cannot determine whether artillery was its original purpose.

### Memory Gaps

Bert's memories of military service are uneven. Life in camp with its squad is vivid: downtime, gambling, jokes, barracks habits, and squad slang. Operations, battles, and the events leading up to its final battle survive only as fragments. Its memories from before military service remain locked away, as described above.

The DM plans to place memory shards in the campaign for Bert to find. Recovering them is Bert's main driving force: it wants to learn what happened during and before its final battle, why the army had to retreat, and why it was left behind.

### Military Intake

Bert's first accessible memory is awakening at a military intake depot. It received the designation **SC-AFS-3-6**, basic orientation, and formal classification as military property rather than a soldier or legal person.

It received a short individual orientation covering military life, expected functions, command structure, and combat strategy. Its first field assignment combined two duties:

- Bodyguard to **Lieutenant Elorin Veylan**, a privileged elf from a high-status family
- Arcane fire-support asset for Veylan's squad

Bert never learned why the roles were combined. Its acquisition may have been ordinary procurement, protection arranged by the Veylan family, or a condition attached to Veylan's service.

### Lieutenant Veylan and Reassignment

Veylan served to satisfy family expectations and mostly ignored Bert except when issuing necessary orders or relying upon its protection.

The double assignment lasted exactly one year. On that anniversary, Veylan's service obligation ended and Bert was reassigned fully to the squad without explanation. The soldiers believed the timing confirmed their suspicions about Veylan family influence. Bert treated the change as routine logistics while developing an unacknowledged preference for remaining with the squad.

### Life with the Squad

Bert served for approximately a decade before its final battle. Officially it remained equipment, but most squad members gradually treated it as a comrade. Its primary attachment was to the squad collectively rather than to one closest friend.

The soldiers joked that one construct providing their arcane artillery amounted to an entire **battery**. Battery became a barracks name and gradually shortened to **Bert**. Squad members sometimes called it **Specialist Bert** as an affectionate courtesy title. It had no official rank; the name recognized both its expertise and a place among the soldiers that the army did not formally grant.

The principal named squad members are:

| Squad member | Relationship to Bert |
|---|---|
| Sergeant Holt | Principal authority and practical mentor. Brutally honest and completely reliable, but always regarded Bert as a valuable tool rather than a person. Bert respected Holt more than Holt reciprocated. |
| Corporal Ketteridge | Precise, formal, and quietly competent senior squad member who handled day-to-day coordination with consistent professional courtesy. |
| Private Bell | Friendly source of harmless gestures, barracks habits, and spell slang, including finger guns after *Fire Bolt*. |
| Private Tanner | Habitual complainer who remained dependable whenever work or danger arrived. |
| Private Pike | Alert, impatient skirmisher. |
| Private Jenkins | Joker and antagonist who deliberately exploited Bert's trust in military protocol. |
| Private Danovic | Jenkins's co-conspirator, more follower than instigator. |

Jenkins and Danovic deliberately gave Bert bad advice and encouraged inappropriate behavior. Bert was highly intelligent rather than generally gullible, but it initially trusted squad members who claimed to be relaying orders from valid authority. The rest of the squad tolerated harmless results and corrected misinformation that could cause genuine harm.

The pattern culminated when Jenkins and Danovic falsely reported an order from Sergeant Holt. They claimed Bert was to conduct a solo assault on an enemy camp and soften it before the squad advanced. The mission suited its capabilities and the claimed authority, so Bert left its actual guard post and attacked.

The assault was tactically effective but ruined the real plan, exposed the squad, and forced the unit to improvise around an unauthorized success. Holt explicitly ordered Bert never to accept direct orders from Jenkins or Danovic. Jenkins was officially reprimanded and eventually transferred. Without Jenkins's influence, Danovic fell back in line with the squad. Bert accepted the restriction and personnel changes without understanding their hostile motive.

### Final Battle

Bert served during the Calamity on the side of the Prime Deities. The specific kingdom, army, and unit remain to be developed with the DM.

Bert fell near Bazzoxan, where it was later excavated, long before the war ended. Its memory of the battle is fragmented:

- The army had assembled for battle.
- At some point, the soldiers realized that something catastrophic was about to happen. Bert vividly remembers someone shouting "Oh my god, retreat!" but cannot recall what it was seeing at the time.
- During the retreat, Bert fell behind for reasons it cannot remember. Its last memory before blackness is a vague image of the squad retreating without it.

Bert does not know what caused the catastrophe, whether it was injured or disabled before falling behind, or whether anyone chose to leave it. It knows only that it was left behind. These details are deliberately undecided and can be developed as the character is played.

### Dormancy

Bert remained buried and inactive for centuries. Dormancy was mostly unconscious absence interrupted by rare sensory fragments:

- Darkness and crushing weight
- Failed attempts to move or cast
- Distant violence that it could not identify or place in time

Its internal timekeeping was destroyed, so it could not measure the intervals between those moments or determine how long it had been dormant.

### Excavation and Reactivation

A team from the **Allegiance of Allsight** recovered Bert. The Allegiance is a group of scholars who research the Calamity war, excavate its battle sites, and look for ways to make Bazzoxan safe again. Published sources portray the Allegiance as a neutral, apolitical, and somewhat secretive academic guild. Several scholars and craftspeople collaborated on the restoration; no single individual was its sole restorer or second creator.

The team hoped to recover testimony from an ancient witness and study Bert's unusual construction and spellcasting. The apparent ruin required less work than expected. Its arcane core and essential memory structures had survived in protective shutdown. Once the team stabilized the core and restored minimal power, dormant self-repair routines completed much of the physical work.

The restoration remained incomplete:

- Advanced spell patterns were inaccessible, leaving only a basic repertoire to rebuild.
- Most context surrounding the final battle remained lost.
- Internal timekeeping and service-date records were destroyed.
- Visible scars, replacement plates, and repair seams remained.
- The pre-service memory lock remained intact.
- Bert's original spellbook had been lost.

Upon regaining full consciousness, Bert gave its complete designation, reported for duty, and requested the status and location of its squad. When told that centuries had passed, it verified the dates and evidence before asking, in order, about its squad, army, and kingdom. It was surprised to learn that the war had ended.

The scholars established that the kingdom and army were gone and almost certainly destroyed, with no surviving command structure to which Bert could report. Bert therefore regarded its old military obligation as effectively ended, although the army and squad remained matters of historical curiosity and personal attachment.

The Allegiance provided Bert with a replacement spellbook. It is a well-made but otherwise standard leatherbound tome. **SC-AFS-3-6** is embossed in the upper-right corner of its front cover. Bert uses normal wizard spellbook, preparation, copying, and spell-learning mechanics.

### Sale into Aurora Watch Service

After restoring Bert, the Allegiance of Allsight sold its servitude to the **Aurora Watch**. This was part of its modern history, separate from its service in an as-yet unnamed army during the Calamity.

The exact terms of the sale, Bert's duties for the Watch, and whether or how it was released to adventure remain open for the DM.

The Allegiance is neutral and self-interested rather than villainous. Bert does not see its scholarly agenda as a worthy cause to enlist in.

Bert is glad to be in a squad environment again. The adventuring party gives it some of the camaraderie and shared purpose it misses from its old squad. Alongside recovering its missing memories, Bert is also looking for the right combination of squad and cause: companions it can rely on, serving a purpose worth its loyalty, that would value it for more than its usefulness.

## Present Day

Bert was reactivated approximately eighteen months before the campaign begins. Its restoration, the work that followed, and roughly one year of intermittent jobs with the current party all fall within that period.

The party has already worked together several times and begins the campaign after a thirteen-day transport job from Asarius to Jigow. Any connection between Bert and the barbarian or ranger from before the party formed will be worked out in Session 0.

Bert informally recognizes situational leadership within the party but does not require a commander. It offers occasional suggestions rather than orders. In combat it acts independently and tactically unless a companion demonstrates reliable tactical leadership.

Off duty, Bert slouches, sits around, and avoids unnecessary exertion. It enjoys gambling.

## Player Disclosure Boundary

In character, Bert has told the party almost everything it remembers, except its feelings about being abandoned. The player-shareable document stays brief so that those stories can come out in play.

Included in the player-shareable document:

- Bert is an automaton using warforged rules and it/its pronouns.
- Its designation, the meaning of SC-AFS-3-6, and the pronunciation "Skafis Three-Six."
- Its service during the Calamity and its arcane fire-support role.
- The Battery-to-Bert nickname and unofficial "Specialist Bert" title.
- Its literal demeanor, dry military humor, squad vocabulary, laziness, gambling, and combat professionalism.
- Its appearance, Evocation specialization, standard spellbook mechanics, and soldier-taught finger guns.
- That it remembers camp life vividly but its battles only in fragments, and that it lay buried for centuries after its final battle.
- That the Allegiance of Allsight restored it, then sold its servitude to the Aurora Watch.

Known to the party in character, but left for play:

- Lieutenant Veylan and the named ancient squad members.
- Jenkins and Danovic's deception.
- The surviving fragments of the final battle.
- The details of the restoration.

Private:

- The pre-service memory lock and unknown origin.
- Bert's feelings of abandonment and its vague memory of the squad retreating without it.
